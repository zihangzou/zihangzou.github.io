I received my Ph.D. from the University of Central Florida, advised by Liqiang Wang.

I study how to protect data ownership and identify plagiarism in neural networks, and how to make learned behavior respect legal and physical constraints. I am currently working on physical scenarios and simulation, with an interest in learning efficiently and adapting over time.

Copyright Protection & Data Plagiarism

My work addresses two related problems: protecting personal data against unauthorized use in model training, and exposing how generative models reproduce copyrighted content. Anti-Neuron Watermarking studies ownership verification, while Neural Plagiarism examines forgery and copyright ambiguity in diffusion models.

Law & Physics Compliance

I study how neural networks can follow legal requirements and physical rules in the scenarios where they operate. This includes deriving driving requirements from traffic laws and using the traffic-flow fundamental diagram to constrain adversarial examples. My current work also builds physically grounded simulation environments, combining vehicle dynamics and interactions between vehicles for reinforcement learning.
